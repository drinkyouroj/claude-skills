# Concept Brief Examples — Worked Cases

Three worked examples covering the most important learning moments for the skill: an inversion (face-forward would have been wrong), a correction (a published cover failed the DNA and what the skill should have produced instead), and an anchor (the success case the skill should pattern-match toward).

The concept-brief **format** lives in `workspace/core/_template-flagship-cover.md` § "Concept brief format". This file is the **teaching corpus** — concrete examples of the format applied well.

---

## Example 1 — The Helium Inversion (face vs. structure)

**Article:** "Helium Operators Built the Network. The Foundation Owns the Multisig."

**Surface read:** named co-authors at the center of the piece. Default move: face-forward, three portrait compositions.

**Correct read:** the argument is about **governance concentration in nominally-decentralized networks**. The named individuals are vectors for the structural critique, not the subject of the critique. A face-forward cover would suggest the piece is *about the people* when it is *about the structure they're embedded in*. Face-forward would mislead the reader.

**Face-gate response:**

> [Names] are at the center of the piece, but the argument is structural — foundation-controlled multisig is the actual subject. Face-forward would mislead. Recommendation: non-face concepts.

**Two strong concepts that emerged (face-gate set to no):**

### Concept A — The seal pressing the network

```
Compression name:      Centralization presses the decentralized
Subject:               A single ornate wax seal, mid-press, on parchment
Secondary element:     A faint network-graph pattern visible UNDER the seal, partially obscured where the seal lands
The relationship:      One signature flattens the network that was supposed to be many signatures
Visual register:       Surreal symbolic illustration
Palette direction:     Warm sepia (the seal, the parchment) + cold blue (the partially-visible network) — color-temperature contrast carrying the institutional/decentralized opposition
DNA checks: 5/5
  ✓ Compression: the seal IS the foundation; the network IS what the seal flattens
  ✓ ≤2 elements: seal + partially-visible network = one unified composition
  ✓ No embedded text: none required
  ✓ Permitted register: surreal symbolic
  ✓ Contrast: warm/cold temperature carrying centralized/decentralized argument
One-line evocation:    A wax seal closes over a network diagram, flattening it where the seal lands.
```

### Concept B — The multisig that isn't

```
Compression name:      Many signatures, one hand
Subject:               A clean isometric multisig icon — multiple signature lines radiating outward from a central dot
Secondary element:     All signature lines, on closer look, originate from the same central point, just rendered as if they were separate
The relationship:      The visual gestalt of "many signatures" resolves, at second look, to "one signature pretending to be many"
Visual register:       Clean technical illustration, isometric
Palette direction:     White background, industrial charcoal + steel blue for the diagram, single warm accent on the central dot — the warm dot IS the foundation
DNA checks: 5/5
  ✓ Compression: the structure of the icon IS the structural argument
  ✓ ≤2 elements: one icon, one center-of-gravity
  ✓ No embedded text: none required
  ✓ Permitted register: clean technical illustration
  ✓ Contrast: cool diagram lines vs. warm center dot
One-line evocation:    A multisig icon where every signature line, on closer look, originates from the same warm center.
```

**Why two concepts, not three:** for this piece, two genuinely different compressions are available — one symbolic (the seal), one structural-iconic (the icon). A third concept would have been a variant of one of these or a weak face-forward fallback. **The skill drops to two rather than padding.**

**Output frontmatter recorded:**

```yaml
face_decision: no
face_subject: null
face_reasoning: "Named co-authors are vectors for the structural critique, not its subject. Face-forward would mislead the reader into reading the piece as about people instead of about governance concentration."
```

## Example 2 — The Process-Is-the-Punishment Correction (system fixing a published failure)

**Article:** "The Process Is the Punishment" (published April 2026).

**What was published (failed cover):** a labeled castle blueprint cross-section with three competing zones — castle + courthouse + calendar — and embedded labels ("moats," "curtain," "supply lines," "provisions").

**Why it failed the DNA:**

- **Check 1 (compression):** the blueprint illustrates the *evidence* (castle metaphor + courtroom + delay) instead of *compressing* the argument.
- **Check 2 (≤2 elements):** three competing zones — castle, courthouse, calendar — all fighting for visual weight.
- **Check 3 (no text):** embedded labels naming castle parts.
- **Check 4 (register):** documentary-scrapbook-adjacent / labeled-blueprint register — both excluded.
- **Check 5 (contrast):** no specific contrast carrying the argument.

**Article thesis:** "the lawsuit is the punishment; the trial is the least important thing." Three compressions are available that all argue the piece honestly.

### Concept A — Wallet bleeding time

```
Compression name:      Time IS the cost
Subject:               A worn leather wallet, photographed close on a dark table
Secondary element:     A clock face reflected in the wallet's metal clasp, hour marks replaced by crossed-off months
The relationship:      The wallet doesn't hold money; it holds the days the case has been running
Visual register:       Photographic with reflection device
Palette direction:     B&W on the wallet/table + one warm-amber spill from the clock reflection — temperature carrying the cost-of-time argument
DNA checks: 5/5
  ✓ Compression: time is the cost; the wallet is the body the cost is paid by
  ✓ ≤2 elements: wallet + clock-reflection
  ✓ No embedded text: the crossed-off months function as marks on a clock, not labels
  ✓ Permitted register: photographic with reflection device (Atlanta-pattern)
  ✓ Contrast: B&W vs. warm amber spill, present (table) vs. reflected (clock)
One-line evocation:    A worn wallet on a dark table; the clock reflected in its clasp counts months, not hours.
```

### Concept B — Two chairs, one absent

```
Compression name:      The defense is being run from the empty chair
Subject:               An empty courtroom photographed in B&W, two chairs at the plaintiff/defendant tables
Secondary element:     One chair occupied by a silhouette, the other empty with visible dust on the seat
The relationship:      The verdict is being decided about a person who cannot afford to be present
Visual register:       Photographic, cinematic
Palette direction:     B&W with one cold-blue accent from a high window — the cold light falls on the empty chair
DNA checks: 5/5
  ✓ Compression: presence vs. absence carries the argument
  ✓ ≤2 elements: two chairs in compositional dialogue
  ✓ No embedded text: none required
  ✓ Permitted register: photographic, cinematic
  ✓ Contrast: present vs. absent — the carrying contrast
One-line evocation:    Two chairs at counsel's tables. One holds a silhouette. The other is dusty.
```

### Concept C — Hourglass paper-flow, verdict below frame

```
Compression name:      The trial never reaches the verdict
Subject:               A clean isometric hourglass on a white background
Secondary element:     Papers streaming through the hourglass's narrow center — never reaching the bottom mark labeled "verdict" (the mark exists as a thin line on the glass, not a label — see exception in Check 3)
The relationship:      The motion of process happens forever; the verdict mark stays out of frame
Visual register:       Clean technical illustration, isometric
Palette direction:     White background, industrial charcoal + steel blue + a single paper-cream stream
DNA checks: 5/5
  ✓ Compression: process flows; verdict never lands
  ✓ ≤2 elements: hourglass + paper-stream
  ✓ No embedded text: the "verdict" mark is a thin line on the glass, not a label — borderline; if AI rendering insists on writing the word, drop the mark and let the empty bottom carry the argument instead
  ✓ Permitted register: clean technical illustration
  ✓ Contrast: motion (papers) vs. stillness (verdict zone), top-of-frame (process) vs. below-frame (resolution)
One-line evocation:    An hourglass where papers flow forever but never reach the verdict mark at the bottom.
```

**Why three concepts work here:** three meaningfully different compressions are available — photographic (wallet), narrative (chairs), technical (hourglass). All three argue the piece honestly. The skill's job is to surface that editorial range, not to deliver one "correct" answer.

**Output frontmatter recorded (for whichever concept the user picks):**

```yaml
face_decision: no
face_subject: null
face_reasoning: "Structural piece about the cost of litigation. No single human anchor; the argument is about the system, not a person."
```

## Example 3 — The $71B Bluff Anchor (template-driven success)

**Article:** "The $71 Billion Bluff."

**Why this is the anchor case:** it demonstrates the preferred prompt-source path (`ai-image-prompts-skill` library lookup) and the strongest example of face-forward being correct.

### The single concept (no need for alternatives — the compression was unambiguous)

```
Compression name:      One signature, falling market
Subject:               Sam Altman in B&W three-piece suit, seated at a long polished black conference table, fountain pen + unsigned document
Secondary element:     A wall of cascading green-lit DRAM memory modules and circuit traces falling like a stock ticker behind him
The relationship:      The decision a single executive holds over a collapsing market
Visual register:       Editorial poster / financial thriller (neo-noir)
Palette direction:     B&W subject + saturated green backdrop — high contrast carrying the "still center / falling world" argument
DNA checks: 5/5
  ✓ Compression: one face, one prop, one backdrop carry the thesis
  ✓ ≤2 elements: figure + falling backdrop
  ✓ No embedded text: none
  ✓ Permitted register: editorial poster
  ✓ Contrast: B&W subject vs. saturated green — the carrying contrast
One-line evocation:    Altman in a B&W three-piece suit at a black table, a fountain pen over an unsigned document; behind him, the entire memory market falls in saturated green.
```

**Face-gate response:**

> The piece is centered on Sam Altman. Should the cover build around his face? Recommendation: yes. The article reports on his specific public-record decision (the $71B LOI) and his face anchors the piece to that specific moment. Public-figure editorial use; senior executive at a major company — well-established editorial territory.

**Prompt-source path taken:** `ai-image-prompts-skill` — returned YouMind #8791 (Neo-Noir Fashion Portrait with High Contrast). The skill mapped:

| YouMind template variable | $71B Bluff value |
|---|---|
| Prop | gun → fountain pen + unsigned document (the LOI) |
| Backdrop | crimson red → saturated green + falling DRAM modules |
| Framing | 4:5 portrait → 16:9 landscape for Substack hero |
| Subject | unnamed model → Sam Altman, B&W three-piece suit |
| Setting | (unspecified) → long polished black conference table |

**Output frontmatter recorded:**

```yaml
based_on: "Neo-Noir Fashion Portrait with High Contrast (YouMind id: 8791)"
prompt_source: ai-image-prompts-skill
compression: "One signature, falling market"
register: "Editorial poster / neo-noir"
palette: "B&W subject + saturated green backdrop"
face_decision: yes
face_subject: "Sam Altman"
face_reasoning: "Named executive in the editorial piece's headline event; his face anchors the piece to the specific public-record decision being reported."
```

## The brief format, summarized

Every concept brief follows the same eight-field structure (from the template doc):

```
Compression name:      [the metaphor strategy in 3-5 words]
Subject:               [primary visual element]
Secondary element:     [the thing the subject relates to]
The relationship:      [one sentence — what carries the thesis]
Visual register:       [photographic / illustrated / isometric / surreal symbolic / etc.]
Palette direction:     [palette choice and why]
DNA checks:            [5/5 — confirmed inline, with one line per check]
One-line evocation:    [the cover described in the voice you'd describe it to a reader]
```

Three concepts means three meaningfully different **compressions**, not three variants of one composition.

Two strong concepts is better than three with one weak. The skill should never pad.
