# TCN Editorial Pillars

The 5 stable pillars that anchor the monthly Substack issue rotation. Order varies month to month based on wiki support and news-cycle pressure; the pillars themselves don't change.

The `30-day-content-plan-{YYYY-MM}.md` frontmatter encodes the month's chosen order in `pillar_order:`. Mode 3's renewal flow uses this list to ensure pillar coverage doesn't repeat narrowly across consecutive months.

---

## Pillar 1 — Monetary Mechanics

**Beat:** Fed independence, monetary instruments vs. supply shocks, dual-mandate inadequacy, liquidity vs. balance-sheet reality, the chokepoints the FOMC cannot price (Hormuz, energy supply, foreign-policy spillover into US inflation).

**Example flagship:** "The Strait Is the Mandate" — Hormuz as the binding monetary constraint, with Warsh's institutional inheritance as the secondary layer.

**Editorial register:** Macro analysis that respects the limits of the instrument. Names which constraints the Fed cannot reach.

---

## Pillar 2 — Power & Infrastructure

**Beat:** AI compute buildout, grid capacity, PPAs, substations, interconnection queues, the physical-infrastructure layer of the AI capex story. Chip-vs-grid concentration tradeoffs. Substations as the chokepoint nobody outside utility-procurement is pricing.

**Example flagship:** "AI's Power Bill — the grid-infrastructure layer of the compute buildout."

**Editorial register:** Infrastructure economics with primary-source rigor — PPA filings, RTO interconnection queues, public utility commission filings.

---

## Pillar 3 — Decentralization Stress Test

**Beat:** DePIN economics from the operator seat, token-vs-operator divergence, the no-token-position moat. Helium, Render, io.net unit economics. Hardware running at negative ROI vs. token speculation.

**Example flagship:** "What Helium Taught Us About Node Operator ROI."

**Editorial register:** Operator-credibility piece, in-body disclosure of operational relationships before any analysis. Token-position-free.

---

## Pillar 4 — Political Systems

**Beat:** Accountability architecture, institutional gaslighting, federalism stress points, civil-rights enforcement gaps, the procedural-clock-running pattern. ICE / immigration enforcement as the documented test case.

**Example flagship:** "3,000 Arrests, 335 Names, One Court Order."

**Editorial register:** Structural argument about institutional patterns, anchored in primary documents (court orders, agency filings, statements on the record). Never partisan jersey framing.

---

## Pillar 5 — Cross-Domain Pattern

**Beat:** Patterns that span the other four pillars. Procurement-as-constitutional-override, vendor-workaround architecture, structural moves visible only across domains. The piece that ties together two or more pillars by naming the shared mechanism.

**Example flagship:** "The Private-Vendor Workaround Goes Federal" — CISA + age-verification + BetterHelp, with Flock (from pillar 4) as the documented predecessor.

**Editorial register:** Synthesis pieces. Higher word count. Usually placed as the month's closer per the strategy's recommendation.

---

## Rotation rules (consumed by Mode 3 renewal flow)

1. **All 5 pillars hit per month if the month has 5 Fridays.** If only 4 Fridays, one pillar is deferred to the next month and noted in the deferring plan's `pillar_order:` frontmatter.
2. **A pillar that produced last month's closer (Cross-Domain Pattern slot) is not eligible for this month's closer slot** — rotate the closer position across pillars over time.
3. **No pillar gets the same Friday position two months in a row.** (E.g., if Pillar 1 was Friday-1 in May, it can't be Friday-1 in June.)
4. **Pillar order is the editorial output, not the input.** Mode 3's Pass 2 decides which shortlisted candidate gets which Friday; `pillar_order:` is written from those decisions, not chosen in advance.
