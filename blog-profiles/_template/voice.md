# Voice Calibration

This file has two sections. The first contains universal anti-AI writing rules that apply to every blog using this system — do not remove or weaken them. The second section is where you calibrate the specific voice of your blog.

---

## Universal AI-tell rules (keep)

These rules apply regardless of blog identity. They correct the most recognizable patterns of AI-generated prose and must be honored by every draft step.

### Banned hollow connectives

Never open a sentence or paragraph with: "Ultimately,", "At the end of the day,", "It's worth noting that", "It's important to remember that", "Needless to say,", "Of course,", "In today's world,", "In the modern landscape". These phrases add zero meaning and signal machine authorship.

### No negative-parallelism constructions

Avoid the pattern "It's not just X — it's Y." This structure sounds like a slogan and produces the kind of abstraction-stacked sentence that appears frequently in AI output. Write the insight directly instead.

### Avoid the vocabulary cliff

Do not mix register within a sentence by abruptly escalating to formal or latinate vocabulary after plain phrasing. Examples of cliff words to avoid unless the rest of the prose matches the register: "plethora", "delve", "paramount", "multifaceted", "nuanced" (when used as filler), "tapestry", "vibrant", "leverage" (as a verb in non-financial contexts), "robust" (when used generically).

### No abstract closing-line summaries

Do not end articles, sections, or paragraphs with a sentence that restates the theme at maximum abstraction — e.g., "This is what makes [topic] so powerful." or "That is the true value of [concept]." Close instead with a concrete implication, a specific next step, or a punchy observation that earns its finality.

### No filler affirmations

Strip any sentence whose only job is to congratulate the reader or validate their interest: "This is a great question.", "You've come to the right place.", "Understanding this topic is crucial for anyone who…". Start with the substance.
